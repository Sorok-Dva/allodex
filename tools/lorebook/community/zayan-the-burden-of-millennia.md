## 12 February, year 2672 since the Creation of the World

How glad I am to be here at last, in the capital, studying at the Ardebrig academy, the world's centre of art and science.

A little about myself: though I descend from one of our kings, a dozen other kinsmen stand between me and any claim to the throne. Power, however, has never interested me. My true passion has always been books. Reading everything I could lay my hands on, I went through my parents' entire library, including not only elven works such as "The Power of Oberon", which tells of the palace intrigues that erupted when the one who is now a Great Mage, and was once a king, renounced the crown.

Or, for instance, I read "The Secrets of Ker-Is by Night", a somewhat frivolous tale of an elven maiden pursued by a bloodthirsty werewolf, and I mastered works describing the lives of other races, such as "Nefertiti and Akhenaten: In the Name of Love", a rather amusing story written by an eyewitness in the last days of Hickut.

I even came by works like "Mechanisation. Volume 3", filled with repulsive details of what that people did to themselves in their attempt to gain immortality.

In my wanderings through the lines of dusty treatises, my faithful companion was my cousin, or, to be precise, my second cousin, Elined.

Oh, Elined, even now, as I write these lines, I see your finely chiselled profile bent over a new folio.

You shared my passion, devouring this knowledge alongside me. It was this very path that brought us here.

Reality has surpassed all my expectations: the libraries of Ker-Is exceed everything I have seen until now. Such a multitude of folios, grimoires and incunabula deserves the closest study. But where to begin?

Without a plan I am doomed to be buried under a mountain of unsorted information, so it is very important to think through what we will take up first. I want to discuss it with Elined; she has always been distinguished by rigorous judgement and a rational approach.

Otherwise, life goes on as usual. I have been presented to our king, a distant relative of mine. The reign of Dernier began not so very long ago, right after the extinction of the previous dynasty, the Velund, from which that same Oberon who renounced the throne descends.

The capital is full of other noble houses too, many of whom pride themselves on their antiquity.

Here, it would seem, one might turn to politics, to relations with the other elven Houses that have founded states of their own; I find that subject rather dull, however.

The reasons for this are simple: the borders of Irene are securely closed by a magical barrier, and those who now reside in the capital are for the most part gilded youth who do not know what to do with themselves. And what could they say about those who live outside?

We, protected by the power of our ancestors from any interference from without, live for the most part in blissful ignorance. All that is left to us is books.

Well, it only remains to find Elined so that we can discuss our prospects, what we want to take up in the near future. Strange, but my cousin has not been seen for a long while...

## 1 June, year 2672 since the Creation of the World

Oh, what an unhappy day! I never thought such a thing could ever happen.

My Elined, what has become of you? How could you renounce our ideals, everything you and I dreamed of, everything we discussed for so long in our library?

The capital is a wonderful place for all who hold knowledge dear, but even here there is no shortage of ruinous places for those who yield to the temptations of an easy life, full of momentary pleasures and empty amusements.

I found my cousin in one such establishment, bearing the name, oh great powers, "Whips and Chains"... There she was making merry, dancing and singing in the company of some good-for-nothings from among those very young scions of the noble Houses. I could only gaze in astonishment as the one I had revered as the cleverest of women in this world kissed some blue-haired flirt full on the mouth, while at the same time offering her unlaced bodice to one of the dandies.

I was simply at a loss for words at such a sight, and only after waiting and catching my breath did I pull myself together and stride firmly towards my merrily laughing cousin. Seizing her by the sleeve, I drew her aside to talk. Here, I must admit, my composure failed me, and I shouted at Elined with all my heart, voicing all my indignation.

I told her everything I thought of her behaviour; she told me everything she thought of mine. And so, word by word, we arrived at some very inelegant insults, when we realised that everyone in the hall was watching us. In parting I received a stinging slap, after which I found no other way out but to leave. In anger I walked through the narrow streets of Ker-Is, not understanding myself where exactly I was going.

My anger was boundless, for I had been betrayed by the one I considered my most reliable ally, the one I had seen at my side in our future endeavours.

A sudden thought came to me, like a flash of lightning. What is the matter? Why are we elves like this? Most of us see nothing else for ourselves but a life of endless pleasures, yet it was not always so. Once our ancestors waged long wars against the world around us, wishing to make this kingdom the safest and most beautiful place in the world. They were far stronger than we are, and lived in far more dangerous times. Their labours have not vanished without a trace; they still surround every inhabitant of the kingdom.

But their descendants have grown much weaker. Pampered. Spineless. But what is the reason? What happened to my people that they changed so? I had no answers, nor were there any in the books I had read before. In them the past was always likened to a forgotten dream, of which only vague sensations remained. Why have we forgotten where we came from? Why do none of our historical works touch upon anything older than a few centuries? And who knows the truth? The Great Mages? I cannot even say with certainty how old they themselves are, but I know that they are now immortal...

Now all of this seemed to me far more important than my frivolous cousin.

## 13 July, year 2672 since the Creation of the World

In search of answers to my questions, I went to the royal library. As a distant kinsman of the king, I had full access to all the archives, even the most ancient. But were there any archives at all? The chamberlain accompanying me, who had been charged with giving me due assistance, unlocked long-closed doors that, it seemed, had not been opened for decades, if not longer. I found myself in dusty rooms where stacks of papers lay in disorder on ancient tables. What a striking contrast with the order in which the main halls are kept, crammed with entertaining, romantic and magical literature. The past of the elves had been thoroughly forgotten.

Sitting down at one of the tables, I began sorting through the papers. Indeed, some of what lay before me were old chronicles, some were records of the palace's needs. But both were of the utmost importance. Alas, the further into the past, the less reliable its traces. Ink fades, paper crumbles into scraps, and of the testimonies of what once was, only dust remains.

Realising that a long task lay ahead, I decided to request an audience with the king. The position of his distant kinsman had its advantages, and I intended to make use of them.

Well, my venture was crowned with success. I managed both to enrol at Ardebrig and to obtain the post of court historian. When I set out my plan to the king, the faces of those around fell. Now, apparently, I am considered an eccentric, if not worse. But I intend, step by step, to get to the bottom of our history, for who are we without what shaped our path in the past?

Every day I trained under the masters of the magical art; every evening I went to the wing of the royal palace given over to me and sorted through the archives that had fallen to my lot.

I very quickly gained the reputation of a rather strange elf who refused the customary pleasures and amusements. I was sent invitations to Balls, and I threw them into the wastepaper basket; I was summoned to yet another important dinner, and I shrugged and returned to my work. My cousin, as far as I could gather from the whispers of my fellow students, had become a very popular person who enjoyed universal adoration, but I did not care. I did not seek to meet her, and the letters she sent me I simply burned unread.

## 2 February, year 2674 since the Creation of the World

So a year passed, and then another. Having finished with the royal archive, I asked the king to issue an order to our Houses that they bring all their old manuscripts and other papers to the new library I had organised.

He was obeyed without any objection, the matter being considered a rather strange whim. For the most part my kinsfolk went on ignoring what I was doing, but in some an interest did arise after all. History in itself still seemed dull to them, but they found in it ideas for their works, drew inspiration from what was described there, devising new paintings and architectural plans, and sought the means of settling ancient lawsuits.

My studies of magic went well: I quickly passed through the degrees of initiation as adept and acolyte, becoming at last a magister. All my free time I continued to spend on the library, gradually moving from putting old manuscripts in order to composing a work of my own. But many difficulties arose along this path, above all because our history remained tragically incomplete. Only fragmentary records bore witness to what had taken place before the Transformation. I found myself at a dead end from which I could as yet see no way out. To tie the scattered threads of history together, I needed to find someone who knew more.

In these gloomy thoughts I was pacing the halls of my library when my gaze fell, quite by chance, on one of the visitors, or rather a woman visitor, bent over one of the volumes. The most unusual thing was that she was not an elf. Before me, beyond any doubt, was a child of the human race. Golden-red hair, delicate features. She was beautiful in her own way. I resolved to speak to her.

"Good day," I greeted her, sitting down at the same table. "Perhaps I can help you with something? The thing is, I am the royal librarian, and, in all modesty, I know the contents of this library better than anyone."

She raised her green eyes to me. Beautiful, she possessed not the ideal beauty that distinguished the elves, yet at the same time something young, fresh and unusual emanated from her.

The human girl, noticing the interest in my eyes, smiled.

"Hello. Yes, perhaps you could help me. I was sent here by the sorceress Maeve, the keeper of Rahl-Ulrir. I would like to find information about my own past."

My confusion and incomprehension must have shown on my face, for the girl hastened to explain:

"My problem is that I do not remember my past. I have lived for more than a hundred years, and apparently I am immortal, but I know nothing of where I come from or who my relatives are... If your library holds any information, I would like to have it."

A riddle indeed. I pondered. Our history is full of vague legends about ancient heroes, about warriors of old, about wanderers who roamed the world... Perhaps there is something about this sorceress too. Gathering my thoughts, I nodded.

"I will help you. I think that together we can solve this mystery. But... how should I address you?"

She tilted her head slightly to one side:

"They call me Bindweed."

## 9 September, year 2678 since the Creation of the World

The longer we live, the less power time has over us. The years quicken their pace, one, another, a third... I myself did not notice how they ran by. Looking back, I understand that it was the happiest time of my life. I had work I loved, I had someone who understood me, I had no responsibilities. I simply lived and enjoyed it.

Bindweed proved to be a good helper. Together we accomplished a great deal, going through every archive there was in Irene. People whispered about us almost openly, but I paid no attention. Unlike many women of my race, this eternally young enchantress was far cleverer and more perceptive.

I tried to help her in every way I could, but in this matter she did far more than I.

There were monstrous gaps in her memory, created by magic of such an order that it is frightening even to imagine. Her first memory was of some field of death, and, if I understood her correctly, it was that very slaughter in which the last of the Hickutians perished.

Bindweed could not tell what exactly had happened there. She remembered only a strange sensation of monstrous darkness, the breath of death itself. She managed to do something so that this contagion would not spread further, and afterwards she buried all the fallen, dragging them away from the sinister Pyramid. One of the dead she remembered especially well, a young warrior in a white mask. She managed to drag them into empty tombs and then sealed the place with magic.

Then she set off north. She met many unusual things along the way, and visited a city high in the mountains, with many tall towers, where some unfortunate people lived who had fled from an unimaginable monster. She believed them and went to seek out the monster, wishing to be rid of it for ever. But on meeting it she again felt the same darkness and, taking fright, fled.

For a long time she wandered through the mountains, saw new inhabitants come to the ruins of abandoned cities, lived among them, then left and went on. So it went until her path crossed that of the Great Mages. But they had seen a great deal and, not particularly surprised by anything, sent her further on. That is how she came to be in Irene.

## 11 November, year 2688 since the Creation of the World

The years passed, and it became ever clearer to us that sooner or later we would have to leave Irene. It was too safe, too unchanging and too sleepy. Real history was being made beyond its borders. I had achieved here everything I could achieve, and it was time to move on. At the same time, I understood that, most likely, our paths would part.

I was close to completing my work, and, sitting by the fireplace, Bindweed read passages from it aloud:

"I cannot vouch for the accuracy of the information I have set down. Time flows like a dark river, and the beginning of history lies hidden in its depths. Much has been forgotten, and still more has been lost. All that remains to us are scraps of old documents and vague memories of what once was."

I sat and listened as she read. The past had always been a riddle to me, and I felt that this mystery was something fateful for all of us. Bindweed spoke of years gone by, of other peoples, of a past in which so much was unknown.

"Thus, after the Change, the history of the elves began anew. The past was left behind, becoming a half-forgotten, happy childhood dream. The elves, on awakening, began their life almost from a clean slate. They tried to forget their former aspirations, surrendering themselves wholly to the present. They were no longer interested in the deeds of their ancestors, their past feats or the chronicles of the War for Beauty. All of that had become meaningless and unnecessary.

That is why there are so many blank spots in the history of the world and the history of the elves. But this is all we have, and there is no one who could tell of the past more than the ancient elven manuscripts of my library, so few of which have survived."

"Maeve could," Bindweed said thoughtfully. "If she knows nothing of my past, that does not mean she knows nothing of your history."

"Perhaps," I remarked. "It is worth speaking to her about it."

"You must speak to her," she said. "Fate itself is leading you down this path."

"And what about you?" I asked, understanding that she was right.

"I will move on. The world is vast, and there are a great many places I have not yet been. I want to go north. They say that beyond the seas there are other lands too. Covered in ice, inhabited by quite different peoples."

In the morning we left Ker-Is, heading through the forest to the magical barrier that separated Irene from the rest of the world. The guards let us through unhindered, and a letter I had sent would carry news of my departure to the king. I did not know how long I would be away.

At last parting became inevitable: the road forked, and our paths too had to diverge. Mine led south, to Ulrir; hers led north, to the Kanian ports on the coast.

We embraced.

"Promise me," she said, "that some day you will fulfil a request of mine."

"My House, in my person, swears upon its honour. Whatever may happen, neither gods, nor elements, nor the spirits of the afterlife shall stop me from fulfilling my duty," I bowed ceremoniously before her, and she burst out laughing. And so we parted.

## 23 December, year 2788 since the Creation of the World

Perhaps I ought to tell of my journey to the tower of Rahl-Ulrir, of my first meeting with the Great Sorceress Maeve and of the details of our conversation; there are, however, things that should not be entrusted to paper.

Only this matters: she invited me to become her apprentice. Well, I must admit that I myself had been considering asking one of the Great Mages to take me as a pupil, but I could not even dream that such an offer would come from someone who sits on the Council of the Conclave. Here an explanation is in order: there are only seven such mages, though the number of Great Mages themselves is far larger.

Rahl-Ulrir is one of the Towers of the Great Mages, each of which bears the name of the region in which it stands, and each of which stands at a crossing of the ley lines that girdle our world. Naturally, a tower can only be built on solid ground, so each such crossing has in the end acquired its tower, except perhaps the most remote and awkwardly placed ones.

The word "Rahl" itself translates as "magic tower". The word comes from one of the New Elven tongues, to whose extraordinary popularity we owe a multitude of modern names and titles. A group of elven poets and writers, even before the Change, concerned themselves with "the beauty of language" and, deeming our ordinary speech insufficiently elegant, zealously set about inventing languages of their own, each more intricate than the last.

The fashion for them spread extremely widely, touching not only elves but humans too. Hence all these ornate names and titles, sometimes devoid of any meaning.

Rahl-Ulrir is the closest of the magic towers to Irene, in effect barring the approaches to it. South of it stands Rahl-Ullah, and beyond that begin the Kanian lands. The story of how these towers came to be is extraordinary. Nothing of the kind existed in the times before the Change; however, when the elves awoke and set about exploring the world anew, they came upon a number of towers located in the centre of the continent. Some of them could in time be entered, but nothing could be learned either about who had raised them or for what purpose. Over time, however, artefacts of extraordinary power were found hidden in the towers. It was suggested that these structures had been raised by the June just before their downfall, but in their architecture the towers resembled nothing they had built before. What is more, each of them had been created in a style of its own, utterly unlike the others.

About a dozen towers were found in all; the entrances to some of them had been broken open even before the elves found them, but most remained intact. It was learned that their walls were protected by unusual enchantments that made the towers highly resistant to outside influence. All this led each member of the Conclave to hasten to occupy a Rahl of their own and begin studying it systematically. Over time the number of mages grew, and then they began building towers of their own, in the image and likeness of these.

Rahl-Ulrir, as it happens, is one of the Rahls that existed from the very beginning. I find it most curious to be here; though I know that over the past centuries it has been explored from top to bottom, it seems to me that it still hides some secrets, some unsolved riddles...

A few words about its mistress: the Great Sorceress is very wise, though from her appearance one cannot tell that she is much older than anyone I have known before. She belongs to a branch of the long-vanished House of Morran, about which almost no records have survived.

After I had been in the tower for several weeks, she honoured me with a conversation and informed me that my training would begin very soon. And though I am ready for new knowledge, I understand that in such a matter patience is what counts.

For now all that remains to me is to explore the tower itself and its library. There are not so many other elves here, and among the servants of our Mistress I have met humans too. If I understand correctly, many of Maeve's former pupils now reside in other elven domains. Alas, until now I have taken little interest in present-day politics, being busy with my archive, and besides, almost no news reaches Irene. My kinsfolk are securely sheltered by the barrier from all the sorrows of the outside world, and they care nothing for what happens in it. But, as far as I now know, the Conclave is in fact actively involved in current politics, working together with human mages.

It only remains to learn more about what has so far been unknown to me. Perhaps the present state of affairs will allow me to better understand the events of the past.

## 20 December, year 2689 since the Creation of the World

Well, a year has passed since the beginning of my training, and I can admit that I have truly achieved a great deal in this time. The most magnificent thing of all, of course, must be acknowledged to be my acquaintance with Maeve herself, this enchanting sorceress, as though stepped out of the times that still draw me to them even now. Feeling like a backward warrior of ancient hosts, I understand perfectly those who in their day committed follies for the sake of her cornflower-blue eyes and golden locks.

Once my people were driven by the idea of beauty, and at the very beginning of their history they would not accept any compromise. Striving for absolute perfection, they created magic, having learned the hidden secrets of this world. Before the Change, magic was the lot of the very best; the selection was strict, and the punishment for a mistake was death.

The elves strove to change the world, ridding it of all Ugliness. It is no wonder that my kinsmen of that epoch surpass many times over those of us who came after them. And my teacher is, of course, the embodiment of all the virtues one could expect of an elf of those times.

Together with Maeve we delved into the secrets of higher magic, even into such forbidden branches as the magic of the mind or necromancy. Such knowledge is necessary above all to counter other mages who practise such arts. In Irene there are strict prohibitions against dark magic, but here, in the rest of the world, the restrictions are much weaker. As my teacher explained, only recently the Conclave faced a situation in which a number of mages from among the humans turned against their own kind. Some power had taken hold of them, and only the efforts of the entire Conclave made it possible to deal with them. Now my teacher and the other Great Mages are busy deciding exactly which knowledge should be passed on to others and which must be placed under strict prohibition.

The greatest trust has been placed in me in that I have been allowed to assist the Council of the Conclave in this work. Now, when the unity of the Council has been shattered by the apostasy of the human mage Nezeb, we must all be on our guard.

## 5 September, year 2690 since the Creation of the World

How remarkable: I have now arrived in the lands of humans, to study under the Great Mage Skrakan, lord of Skytower. The road to this forest country was rather long, though fairly safe. The Kingdom of Umoir is one of the many human domains that only formally submit to the authority of Emperor Valir. As far as I know, several elven Houses have also found refuge in Skrakan's lands, so it will be most interesting for me to talk with my kinsfolk. Perhaps what I manage to learn from humans and from elves far removed from Irene will allow me to complete my "Chronicles of Times Past".

Skrakan is an amazing man. In appearance he is already advanced in years, as is typical of humans, who age slowly over the course of their lives; yet, like the other Great Mages, he is very wise and observant. He at once grasped the essence of my requests and was most benevolently disposed towards me throughout our conversation. Skrakan told me the substance of the matter on which Maeve had sent me to him. Originally, after the war with the orcs, the human mages took on many pupils, wishing to increase the number of Great Mages quickly; over time, however, the shortcomings of this approach became clear.

Now the Conclave intends to hold an assembly to work out new rules for those whom the members of the Conclave teach. It seems each Great Mage will be allowed to teach no more than one pupil at a time. In addition, obtaining the very title of Great Mage will be made harder, and the test a candidate must pass will become more difficult.

Skrakan showed me his astronomical instruments. The old man is greatly taken with the problem of studying the so-called "outer worlds", and, if I understand him correctly, he believes that there is some connection between our world and others. At Ardebrig such theories were considered purely speculative, but Skrakan supports his version with certain observations. A curious picture emerges from his charts: it seems that our pole star and the constellation of twelve stars surrounding it may be linked to the vectors of the field of ley lines that converge at the poles of our world.

He could say more precisely only once he had fitted out an expedition to one of the large islands lying in the polar region. That archipelago is close enough to the point in question; such an undertaking, however, requires long and thorough preparation. It is inhabited by very unusual races whose bodies are covered with fur, and the animals there are much larger and more dangerous than those that live on Yul.

Alas, his present affairs do not allow him to take up the question. The human Conclave needs guidance as never before.

## 7 January, year 2743 since the Creation of the World

My work devoted to the history of the elves is finished. History itself, however, is not finished, and therefore we still have something to tell. Though the Kingdom of Irene has frozen in drowsy oblivion, there are still many other states, both human, beginning with the Empire of Kania itself, and elven, founded by those who left their native lands.

Under the wise guidance of the Conclave, in which the mages of elves and humans alike have united, these states may in future achieve a great deal.

I too am now numbered among the Great Mages, for I have passed the test, performed all the necessary rituals and become the owner of a tower of my own.

Rahl-Ney lies in a fertile valley in the mountains. The mountains of Gipat separate it from the main body of the Kanian domains, and these parts have only begun to be settled. Of the previous owners only ruins remain, among them one of the still-working portals that link the former provinces of the June state.

The tower itself was built by Kanians, but it is quite convenient for my purposes. First of all, I ordered that all the manuscripts I had collected be brought here. It still remained to send someone to Irene to make copies of documents in the royal library. Perhaps I too will need pupils, but I have not yet seen suitable candidates.

My musings about future plans were interrupted by the arrival of unexpected guests. The Great Mage Tensess was known to me, but I had never met his companion. Inviting them into the guest chambers, I prepared myself for an interesting conversation.

Tensess is one of Skrakan's pupils and is in effect the head of Kania. And although formally all power belongs to Emperor Valir, the voice of the Great Mage of Kania has always been decisive in settling any question. Tensess went straight to the point:

"As you already know," he said, after the usual ceremonies of greeting, when we had settled at a comfortable table, "Skrakan once had two pupils. Myself and Nezeb. Nezeb was like a brother to me, but his lust for power and his unwillingness to come to terms with others forced me into the duel everyone knows of. He left me no choice, and after his defeat he was exiled to the southern deserts. We are watching him in secret."

Of course, I knew of the memorable duel between the two human Great Mages. Conflicts like this one had occurred among the elves too; it was not for nothing that the first schism in the Council took place even before humans became full members of it.

"Nezeb has found a great deal in the lands of the south," said Tensess's companion, whom I had never seen before, joining the conversation. "Those parts concealed some very remarkable creatures. Jinn, and not only them. The tribes with which Nezeb has allied himself now worship him, and he in turn has taught them magic and created a Conclave of his own."

For some time I wondered who this mysterious companion might be. She wore a long grey cloak with a wide hood, and her face remained in shadow.

"Nezeb has not given up his dreams of power. I wager that sooner or later he will try to return to Kania. This time as a conqueror," Tensess shook his head. "Sevia and I," he indicated his companion, "have long considered what to do. Nezeb must not gain power over Kania. Perhaps we should cede him part of the lands. Let him play the wise ruler and solve his subjects' problems himself. Seeing how he rules, even his most devoted supporters here, in Kania, will abandon him."

"Part of the lands, together with those who live on them?" I was, to be honest, surprised. "I do not think they would wish to find themselves under a conqueror's rule. Is there truly no way to stop Nezeb?"

"War is inevitable," Tensess sighed. "And however much I regret it, Nezeb can hardly be stopped at our borders. But once he invades the Empire of Kania, he will be forced to leave garrisons in the cities he captures, which will certainly weaken him. Besides, I have no other means of uniting the Kanians themselves. Only when they feel a threat to their own domains will they act together. There is no other way, and so we shall have to sacrifice the few for the good of the many."

For some time I pondered this idea. It would never have occurred to my countrymen, though for the most part those who remained living in Irene had never been interested in those who lived beyond the barrier.

"Nezeb," Sevia went on, "is still gathering his armies. He is immortal, and therefore in no hurry. We can prepare, and though Valir is not ready to listen to us, the other Great Mages will prove, at the decisive moment, to be the force that stops him. We have a difficulty, however, and that is why we decided to turn to you. There is something Nezeb must not obtain. I speak of an artefact of extraordinary power that was hidden in one of the towers."

I did of course already know of these artefacts; indeed, I had even held one of them in my hands: the Key of Creation, carefully guarded by my teacher Maeve. The Radiant Beacon had been entrusted to Oberon; Skrakan himself had the Stone Flower; I know too that one of these artefacts was found far to the east, in the lands of the orcs, where some of my kinsfolk had settled. Two keys were now considered lost.

And now another one has been found in the lands of the south. I had always supposed that there were more of these magical objects, for some towers had already been broken into when my awakened ancestors discovered them.

"You think that with this artefact Nezeb will become much stronger?" I asked.

"Other such objects have caused us a great deal of trouble," Tensess remarked. "In a sense, I am even glad we are rid of them. Rada and Rod, their obsession, the powers available to them... All of it is frightening. And the cause of it all was the artefacts they obtained. They must be handled as carefully as possible. And Nezeb... was never careful."

With that I could only agree. Skrakan's pupil was known as a dangerous madman whose plans were ambitious and whose means of achieving his ends were not always clean.

"The artefact must be taken out of the southern lands before Nezeb and his pupils find it," the Great Mage of Kania continued. "I want you to go there, to the lands of the south, and bring this artefact to me. Sevia will go with you; she knows the domains of my former friend well. I think the Conclave can trust you. There is now one vacant seat on the Council, Nezeb's seat, and you could have it."

## 8 January, year 2743 since the Creation of the World

We left Rahl-Ney at dawn. Mighty lions, creatures of magic, carried us forward. The way was long, but I hoped that the ancient June gates would help us shorten it: by my calculations one of the portals led somewhere into the desert, right into the heart of Nezeb's domains.

The June Mountains are endless chains of peaks stretching through the centre of the continent, separating east from west and north from south. Snow-white ridges alternate with green valleys, in one of which my new home stood. It is here that all the great rivers are born, and many caves lie hidden at the roots of the mountains. How unlike Irene, covered in bright forests, where there is nothing higher than small hills and the cliffs on the coast.

For a while we were silent, but then I decided to address my companion, about whom I still knew nothing.

"You are not one of the Great Mages," I remarked cautiously, wondering how to begin this conversation.
