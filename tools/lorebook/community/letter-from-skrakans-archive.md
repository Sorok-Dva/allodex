## A Letter from "Skrakan's Archive". A Message to Zayan from the Great Mage Skrakan

Written in Skytower, in the year 2741 since the Creation of the world

"Zayan, my dear friend, I am glad to hear the good news that you are in good health and close to completing your monumental work. I was pleased by the question you asked me in your last letter. So, you wish to know everything we know about the northern continent known as 'Isa'. First of all, let me note that the information we have is extremely fragmentary; most of it consists of myths and legends, second-hand retellings, and no one will vouch for its absolute reliability.

Isa is separated from our continent, Yul, by boundless seas and oceans. The northern expanses of water are difficult to navigate, full of reefs and floating mountains of ice. Only a few have ever managed to sail as far as the lands hidden behind these many obstacles.

What do we know for certain? Long ago, back in the age of the June, the orcs, hard pressed by the dragons, withdrew to the eastern edge of their steppes. There, on the shore, they managed to build ships, and some of them set out away from Yul. The voyage was hard, but in the end, having lost many ships along the way, they landed on the shores of that very Isa.

The orcs were astonished. The new lands were far harsher than their homeland. Isa is a land of fire and ice. Countless volcanoes alternated with age-old glaciers; mountain valleys full of greenery broke off into narrow fjords where furious winds howled.

Just as fierce were those who inhabited these lands: every animal here possessed an extraordinary vital force that made it much mightier, larger and shaggier. The orc shamans believed that for some reason Isa possessed a special energy, a kind of primordial power that filled everyone born there. Over time the orcs themselves grew stronger than the kin they had left behind in the steppes.

But besides the many beasts, there were intelligent races here as well. History has not preserved their names for us, but the orcs pass on by word of mouth that they were numerous, fierce and knew no mercy. They particularly remembered certain creatures resembling goblins, small in stature but incredibly formidable as warriors. These merciless little ones guarded a huge Tree that grew in the very centre of Isa. And although the orcs made many attempts to cut it down, these frenzied, fur-covered savages thwarted every one of them. With a hidden fear they are afraid to admit, the orcs speak of three brothers who in the end managed to gather a vast army from all the races that inhabited Isa. This countless host of ruthless warriors besieged the orcs' own settlement and forced them to flee Isa. The orcs returned to Yul, and those who survived founded the clan of the Northwinds, from which, as you probably know, came that very Skulnar, chieftain of the Horde, who was defeated by Tensess and Nezeb.

As for the local frosts, here we can turn to the records of the June. From what we have been able to decipher, we know that the June believed many gods dwelt in the north. Perhaps the June learned some things from the dragons. The dragons were always extremely wary of the northern lands and, by all appearances, watched closely what went on there.

The June believed that this unnatural frost was caused by one of the gods, whom they had once summoned to one of their cities, after which that city froze to its very foundations. So it is on Isa too: the frosts can kill anyone who does not find a way to keep warm. They are especially cruel in the mountains, where, according to the orcs' legends, one may meet ice maidens, servants of this very god of cold.

The way the locals escape the frost is interesting: besides blazing fires, they collect animal fat and also brew special intoxicating drinks from honey that warm you even in the bitterest cold.

What else can I tell you? Alas, we know less than I would like. Perhaps, if I or some other Kanian manages to go there, we shall learn more. Our Circle of Druids has taken an interest in the tale of this enormous Tree. They dream of one day obtaining its seeds and then planting them on Yul.

Perhaps, unlike the orcs, we shall be able to find common ground with the locals?

It is said that they believe in all-powerful fate: that everyone's life is written down before their birth, and what must be, will be. They are superstitious and believe in many signs and omens. It is said that they are ruled by certain seeresses who read the future in woven cloths. These little ones are even convinced that sooner or later the end of the world will come... The Great Nothing will come, they say, and swallow most of the world; everything that exists will perish, but thanks to their Tree they will survive. Of course, to us Great Mages, who know how this world is arranged, such things seem laughable, but we must treat such prejudices with respect if we wish to establish contact with the locals.

Alas, that is all I know about Isa. Not much, truly, but I hope it will help you complete your work."
