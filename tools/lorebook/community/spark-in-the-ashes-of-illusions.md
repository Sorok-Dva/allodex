"Before me once again lies the bog, soaked in rot and ancient magic. The smoky shadows of the vaults close in like the ribs of a giant beast. I remember... We were marching to our deaths. Olga with her dragon's stubbornness, Zayan with his icy reasoning, Bargest, a hound snarling at fate. And him: the Forest God, a tyrant god who weaves circles of cycles out of other people's Sparks and suffering.

Finist vanished first. His falcon circled over the marshes like a harbinger. By the time we passed the Fifth Vault the old man was already raving, but he managed to whisper: 'They will not forgive us for this, Lik.' I nodded. Forgiveness is the lot of the weak.

Olga was eager to burn the vaults, not understanding that the flame is only a curtain before the main act. I carried another weapon within me: a vial of murky slime. Tep's Plague. The irony: using the poison of a mad necromancer to crush a god. The Forest God collects Sparks? Let him try to digest this.

The Sixth Vault demanded loyalty. I smirked. I am loyal to one thing only: the end. When the vault collapsed, Zayan threw me a look full of old hatred. He knew. He had always known that I choose extremes.

In the clearing by the throne the Forest God met us with silence. Rada, his newly regained queen, was whispering something to Finist. The tracker was trembling, clutching a knife, a gift from Tep. The fool. He believed that his daughter's death could be bargained with the gods.

The battle... Olga fell defending Kaah. Zayan, my eternal rival, took the blow of the Forest God's claws in my place. I smashed the vial. The black slime crept along the roots of the throne. 'You are no god,' I hissed at him. 'You are a parasite stuck in your own cycles.'

Rada screamed, recognising the plague. The Forest God pierced me with magic, but I could already see it: his flesh was pulsing, soaking up the contagion. Victory. Even as I died, I laughed.

And then... A break in the clouds. Rain. The god stood hunched, and Rada was stroking his shoulder, as if comforting a child. 'You are not ready,' he said. 'But I am tired of being your cage.'

Finist was sobbing, Koschei was cursing, Olga was demanding answers. And I, a ghost in my own dream, watched them go off to new battles.

Three times I died. Three times I returned. But now it is the end. No, not quite. A particle of me has remained in the plague, in the earth, in their memory.

'A sacrifice?' the wind whispers. 'Or a calculation?'

I do not answer. The dreams of the dead are brief."

The shadows of the marshes dissolved. I open my eyes: the metal vaults of the Wireframe project are as cold as Tep's gaze. My palm reaches involuntarily for my chest, where the plague has left a burning reminder. "Death in a forest on Horizon." Was the dream... an insight? Or a mockery of Ananke?

On the table before me lies the Eidos of Nihaz. The cube flickers like a captured star. "You are his mirror," a voice from the dream hisses in my memory. A lie. Tep clung to predestination, whereas I...

I stand up. In the mirror on the wall is a face I hate. An image of the past distorted by pain, the ghost of a time when I was alive. I put on the mask, my pale and flawless visage. Head of the Order of Creation. The irony of the title. To create through destruction.

"You are afraid," I tell the reflection. "Not of death. Of the fact that she is right."

My hand clutches the Eidos. Pain pierces phantom bones: it still rejects me. As they all do. "A symbol of fear." No. I am a Spark in the ashes of their illusions.

I walk to the window. There, around the tower, Xadagan swarms. Eternal children in the mud, peddlers of lies, soldiers with empty eyes. The chain of Ananke rings on their necks, invisible, eternal.

"Freedom demands sacrifice," I whisper.

I remember the forest. The black roots of the plague sinking into the Forest God. "You are a parasite." But am I not the same? I feed on their fears, their hopes...

I jerk my hand away from the Eidos.

"No. I go further than he did."

I open the casket of relics. A vial with the remnants of the Plague: the madman's last gift. I laugh. Tep believed he would destroy me with this poison. He did not understand: I am already infected. Not with plague, but with an idea.

"You were wrong," I say to the emptiness. "It is not the chains of Ananke that must be broken. It is reality itself."

Holograms of the allods flicker on the wall. Millions of lives... digital. Ghostly. "You erase them like mistakes." No. I set them free.

But my fingers tremble. Weakness? No. Cold calculation. Even gods have doubts.

On the threshold I turn around. The Eidos pulses in time with my heart.

They do not understand. Inhabitants of a prison woven from fear. Every algorithm is a chain, every virtual dawn a lie, an imitation of the sun for those who have never seen the real one. The New Reality will not destroy them... it will melt them down. Like a smith reforging swords into ploughshares.

"Will gardens grow from the ashes?" I ask the emptiness. Tep would have laughed. He saw eternity in Sarnaut. I see clay.

We are all puppets dancing to the whisper of Ananke. The New Reality will give them a sky without illusions, a land that the Astral does not gnaw at. Let the first generations weep, wandering in the chaos of freedom. Better the pain of growth than the oblivion of predestination.

The Eidos pulses brighter. Nihaz... were you afraid too? Your Eidos is a key, not a relic. You will open the door, even if it kills you.

"They will call it the end of everything," I tell the sphere. "But an end is only a beginning."

I remember the dead children from the time of Hickut. Their eyes, empty like the screens of extinguished terminals. In the New Reality they will not beg for mercy. They will become demiurges. They will shape mountains from their own dreams, rivers from their tears. Yes, the first waves will drown. But those who survive...

The Conclave will call it heresy. The Great Mages will scream of betrayal. Let them. When Ananke the Weaver tears the cloth, the Threads twist into a new shape.

"Do you want to be a God?" my reflection asked this morning.

"No. I will be the fire in which they burn."

What will crawl out of the ashes is not utopia but barbarism, beautiful in its imperfection. They will fall, scrape their knees on reality, get up and walk on again. Without scripts. Without "quests". Without a fate sewn into their Spark.

The holograms of the allods go dark. In their place: emptiness. Pure. Whole.
