*The truth is somewhere near.*

Agent Makalani looked thoughtfully at his report, glimmering in green letters on the monitor screen. The Committee would, as always, be displeased with his conclusions. Rysina would shout and pound the table, but he had no others for them. Many years had gone into fruitless searches, into chasing ghosts. They had constantly thrown spanners in his works, but he had not given up and had carried on with his work.

For a long time he had asked himself: "Why?" Why had the mighty and merciless machine of the Empire not finished him off? Perhaps the whole thing was that he had mysterious patrons who had more than once rescued him from seemingly hopeless situations?

This time, however, they seemed to have abandoned him… Long months of attempts to unravel the mystery, or even to come close to it, had led nowhere. Brooding darkly, he read through his report once more:

"The Arisen community on the allod of Xadagan has its own peculiarities, which sometimes baffle those accustomed to other members of the Zem people. It is, however, widely known that Nefer Ur himself divided his kinsfolk into three groups — absolutists, progressors and mystics. For many, no doubt, the image of an Arisen standing at a laboratory bench, broadening the horizons of knowledge, has become more or less canonical. Consider this: to us, that is exactly what the closest followers of Ur himself are like — those whom he called the ones who believe that 'the future of the world must not depend on chance'.

"But there is another part of the Zem people, who believe that there is nothing more to strive for, that their state is already close to the absolute ideal, that time has stopped, and that it was only luck — precisely a chance coincidence of circumstances — that allowed them to gain immortality. They are decadents and pessimists, for whom life is a memory of the past.

"Yes, the peoples of Sarnaut know the first well, but of the second… of the 'absolutists' they know almost nothing. Xadagan, however — the steel heart of the civilisation of reborn Hickut, where the noisy city of Sar-Iram is home to a large population consisting mainly of reanimated dead — will present you with precisely them, as the dominant group.

"But did you really expect that all the resurrected would forget how they had once been alive?"

He glanced back at his companion. Her charm was so… flawless? Perfect grey skin, inlays of implants and augmentations, for clothing only an elegant mask, limbs of blackened metal. She was perfection of her kind. What a pity that she was merely assigned to him, to watch his every move.

Every morning she left with a jeb-card hidden by her tail neuroshunt, onto which, while he was not looking, she dumped the information he had gathered.

He went on reading his report, paying no attention to her greedy gaze. Of course, she was devoted… to that man. The one whose name was hidden under the strictest ban. The one of whom even the functionaries of the Committee knew nothing, for even the watchmen must be watched by someone.

"Nor do the absolutists form a single community bound by mutual interests. They break up into many groups, each with its own aims. On Xadagan several of them can be distinguished.

"The first of these I will call the 'hebets', also known as 'henens', members of a subculture that is gradually penetrating every layer of the Empire. Their origin can be traced to the last years of Hickut's existence, a time when the technologies created by mechanics made it possible to build very complex technical devices. These instruments, which taken together can be designated 'jeb-tech', serve to display a kind of illusory reality on holographic screens or as images transmitted directly into the brain. An example of 'jeb-tech' of an extremely high level was the world of illusions created by Tep, but even more primitive models are highly alluring. The hebets spend all their time on the jeb-net or at jeb-devices, playing and amusing themselves without breaking off for any other occupation. They also organise various tournaments, for which the hebets gather in clubs specially equipped for them.

"The second group I would call the 'kheni'. The kheni are avid listeners of musicians belonging to various musical movements. The bards of Xadagan constitute a separate and highly respected body; however, owing to their critical attitude towards the Empire and its policies, they are not represented in the army, unlike, say, the agitators and touring performers. Here these bards are called 'shemau', and every concert they give draws crowds of fans. Depending on the shemau they revere, however, the kheni themselves divide into separate categories. At present the two most popular factions are the sekhems, who have accepted their death and made friends with it, and the iahkebs, who try to imitate the emotions they have lost by exaggerating their expression.

"Among others worth mentioning I must name the 'haisatkas', once priestesses and now dancers, who attract those Arisen whose minds still cling to lost pleasures that are no longer available to them as they were to the living. The delights of the flesh are now replaced for them by a rich imagination, and the abundant products the haisatkas make now adorn the walls of Sar-Iram everywhere. They also make active use of the jeb-net, offering whole sessions to lured viewers ready to part with their last money for it.

"Having mentioned these shameless but law-abiding maidens, I am compelled to move on to those who are no longer so harmless. The Committee may know them under the general name of the Zem Heretics, whom we have encountered more than once outside Xadagan as well. But here this criminal gang has managed to take root and even to begin challenging the authority of Nefer Ur himself. The fact is that the heretics are in truth the real rulers of Hickut, overthrown in the course of an uprising that ended in success shortly before Tep's plague. Now these once-powerful priests and members of the nobility dream of regaining their own power.

"Into their ranks they now recruit a special caste among the Arisen — the so-called 'robobeasts', adepts of old cults and devotees of additional augmentations. Among them are the 'youngest' members of the Zem people, ready to do anything to increase their own strength, even agreeing to go down on all fours and become like beasts.

"Still, there are allies of Nefer Ur among the nobility too — thus the Khadmon family, which owns its own mansion in the city and has long overseen the funeral rites and burials in Hickut, is loyal to him. Now their responsibility extends to restoring the bodies of the newly reborn and to supervising those bodies that have not gained a Spark of their own, for which they cooperate with the local branch of the Necro-Incubator.

"However, by no means all the 'absolutists' give themselves over to entertainment — to one more group belong the kahuteis, manual workers employed both at the local quarry and in the astral port. The kahuteis disapprove of everyone else, especially those who do not work with their own hands in the plants and factories, those engaged in purely intellectual work. All of these they in turn call 'shepeseis', and the confrontation between the 'kahuteis' and the 'shepeseis' grows with every day.

"As for scientific projects, a great many are under way on Xadagan, from the development of a new type of portal network to biofarms where biomorphs are bred. I will, however, draw the Committee's attention to a few of them that are of particular interest.

"First of all, there is the library-archive, a collection of information about everything in the world. The current head of the archive, Negus Menes, and his companion, Nomarkh Miu, are extremely absorbed in the history of Hickut's last months and in the personality of Tep. Rumour has it that Negus Menes has managed to dig up extremely inconvenient facts that could cast a shadow over the entire civilisation of the Arisen.

"Another project is carried out by Negus Kambizet, who runs the local astral power station, the XAES. At the XAES there is an experimental high-potential reactor; however, for unknown reasons, in recent months its power has not been enough to supply all of Xadagan without interruption. There is talk that the problem may lie in the power being consumed — but on what exactly is it spent? Some believe it is used to mine jeb-hedge, which is computed on nefet-devices; others say the shemau and their equipment are to blame. But I have a theory of a different kind, which I am not yet ready to present to the Committee — first I need to get into the underground premises beneath the station in order to understand what exactly Nefer Ur's staff installed there not long ago.

"If you are interested in the 'robobeasts' I mentioned above, I can report with full responsibility that they came into being thanks to Negus Pilis, head of the Dome project, which conducts research into protoblood — thanks to which we, the Arisen, are able to function — and into the possibilities of interaction between the living and the non-living. The Dome is a closed institution whose experiments are very dangerous, and emissions from this facility have caused large-scale mutations in what fauna remains on Xadagan.

"Nefer Ur himself is at present occupied with the Wireframe project, which is somehow connected with possible protection against Tep. According to my information, the Wireframe project, located right in the centre of the city, is in some way meant in the future to neutralise the threat from this God of Death, and from the other potentially dangerous gods as well. At present the Wireframe is still far from completion, and Nefer Ur is entirely focused on bringing it about, having handed all the administration of Sar-Iram over to Negus Shuponu.

"Finally, the project that in my view is key, led by Negus Jibayd — a project of near-space flights beyond the astral. This long-developed programme should in the end make it possible to reach Sarnaut's natural satellite, the moon, which is believed to have survived the Cataclysm. At the moment the first orbital flight is being planned, to be followed by others…

"The greatest deed, about which the other peoples of Sarnaut will compose legends."

He put down the final full stop. He could say nothing more, but that was not all. It remained to find a way to shake off the surveillance and pass on the necessary information.

"I want to take a walk…" he said casually, pressing the button to send the letter. Her glowing green eyes followed his every move. Let her stay here and rummage through his things; there had to be another way of making contact.

He went out into the street. Sar-Iram was crowded and noisy, as always, every minute of its existence. He would find a way to deliver the message.

Agent Sarang Makalani stepped into the street and vanished into the crowd. A little later someone's hand slipped a note into the gap between a wall and an old holoprojector. And had there been anyone who could read what was written, they would have seen the lines:

"Everyone lies.

"The Cosmodrome is only a cover.

"They are already here."
