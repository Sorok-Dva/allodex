## 13 March 833

I was fortunate to be born into a family of rulers, and it was truly a great stroke of luck. I cannot even imagine myself as a peasant girl milking cows and digging in the earth, or as a merchant's wife counting coins. To stand above that rabble — that is the greatest happiness.

And yet even blue blood does not guarantee that all your wishes will come true. My parents, Radan and Obrena of Scarge, are very distant kin of the ruling dynasty. To my regret, our allod is one of the most out-of-the-way places in the world. Even before the Cataclysm it was a backwater province, and now real politics is made anywhere but here.

My lot is to become the next ruler of this allod, since I am the only child in the family. Of course, there are all sorts of distant relatives, but they need not be taken into account. It is precisely because of my future role that my father now makes me study all manner of sciences so diligently.

But I dream of something else entirely. From birth I understood that my destiny is to rule over others, but Scarge is not enough for me. I would like to be… an empress at the very least, but that is possible only if the eldest prince, the son of Emperor Valir, asks for my hand. It is foolish to count on that, I suppose, but what else is there to do?

## 13 February 835

Today's incident was the most unusual of my whole life… My parents and I travelled to the capital, to the Council of Nobles. Of late certain hotheads have been stirring up trouble, as my father says. And it would not hurt us to visit the fair, where one can buy many interesting things.

And it was at the fair that it happened… My nanny had just bought me some sweets when suddenly the cobblestones beside me cracked and stones sprayed in all directions. The people — foolish serfs, cowardly peasants — of course started screaming and ran, trampling one another, and knocked me off my feet. And there I sat on my backside in the mud, mouth agape, watching what was happening. Out of the hole that had opened as the earth split apart, a dead man was crawling. All in rags, his eyes burning.

Then even I could not hold back; I screamed with all my might, thinking my hour of death had come… I stretched out my hand to shield myself from him… And still he kept crawling, staring at me with that mask of his, that loathsome mug… He was even rasping something, only I could not make it out. Terrifying!

Then a fiery bolt of lightning leapt from my hand and struck the monster. I myself did not understand how it happened. Then my father and his servants came running, and with them the guards of Rahl-Scarge. They took me, frightened as I was, by my lily-white hands and led me to the Great Mage, wise Smaragd.

It turned out that I, too, have a gift — I can become a sorceress. If I complete my training, of course. My father was saddened by this, for he had wished another path for me.

## 10 April 836

My fate has taken another sharp turn.

My father, my mother… Now I shall never see you again. All of it is the fault of envious villains who plotted to destroy our princely line. And worst of all is the sorcerer Hyster, a pathetic charlatan now sitting in Yargrad. It is to him that I owe my present position.

There is nothing worse to say. I was a noblewoman and I have become a wretched servant. And to whom! That witch — not for nothing do they call her the Hag. She lives deep in the bogs where only outlaws go. She welcomes all sorts of thieves, supplies them with potions, keeps their hiding places. As for me… all that is left to me is to clean and do the washing.

I tried to get help from those brigands, but it brought me nothing but pain and humiliation. The chieftain laughed at me and drove me away after I had come to him of my own free will.

At least those louts no longer look my way! Not that there would be anything to look at; I would not recognise myself now. No braids, my hair a tangle like a crow's nest with straw in it, my face dark with dirt.

If only I can endure, if only I can survive this, I will take revenge on everyone — they will sit on stakes and writhe in agony. I have a plan, and if it works, I will escape. I have made a little doll, and not a simple one but an enchanted one. The body is a stick, the arms are made from a broom, and in place of a head there is a pumpkin. It will be my faithful helper.

## 5 June 839

The time spent with the accursed witch is forgotten; it has been three years since I left her service, having regained my name and my appearance. Now I am a sorceress, one of the faithful pupils of Smaragd himself. Hyster has fled, and the rebels who destroyed my family are all dead.

My uncle now rules the allod — not my father's brother but a distant relative, yet a faithful and capable servant. And Smaragd, though old, knows more than anyone could ever learn even if they lived a thousand years. My teacher studies not only the magic of the elements, which the elves call "elements", but also all kinds of clever devices invented in ancient times. He says that the first were the June, who created ingenious golems; after them came the people of Hickut, who replaced flesh with metal. And the elves also made a good many things.

But Smaragd does not only study antiquities; he builds all sorts of things himself. So when the vile Hyster was plotting his coup, the old man was busy with his project: near Yargrad he was building yet another city, called Chasograd. None of the living dwell there, only those whom Smaragd himself created. There are mechanical animals there, and birds as well, but above all people. They walk and talk just like the living, although this happens thanks to all sorts of little springs and gears inside them. One only has to turn a little key and they live. Of course, magic played its part here too.

Although the other pupils say that I am unrestrained and stubborn, the old man did see something in me… My story interested him when I told him that I had brought a scarecrow to life. He understood that I had a talent, and that this talent could be developed.

## 2 October 845

My training is coming to an end. One last, most important step remains. Representatives of the Conclave have already gathered for the trial. Few can leave their own allods, so the company is small but distinguished: Friard of Lightwood, Plamen of Ovus, Potifar of Malar, Falacer of Ellanesh, Bernard of Uramesa and Catherine of Andis. The worst of them, of course, is that elf woman — a cold bitch who is several hundred years old. They say she hates humans, and sorceresses most of all. I was the first woman of my race since the Cataclysm to dare to become a Great Mage. My teacher, Smaragd, was here with me too.

The Test is no joking matter. It is the rite of subjugating the elements, for the elements are the essence of sorcery. But besides them, the ritual will draw on both high necromancy and the magic of the mind. No one can say exactly what they saw; no one knows what ghosts of past times appeared to them.

I entered the circle dressed in a simple robe of coarse cloth. This was our spellcasting hall, hidden in the depths of Rahl-Scarge. An empty place finished in unpolished granite. Only the circle of those standing was lit; beyond it darkness reigned.

"Since time immemorial it has been held that four elements lie at the foundation of the universe. They are called earth, air, fire and water," came the voice of my teacher.

"Since ancient times mages have sought ways and means to control these elements. They can be governed directly. Pour water into a jug, make the air turn mills, sow grain in the earth, forge tools with the help of fire. But a mage's instruments are different. They are reason, thought and will."

"On this night you, Catherina, have come to us to be initiated into the mystery. You strive to control what is beyond the strength of most. If magic deems you worthy, it will grant you abilities that no one but the Great Mages can command. We have gathered here to learn the answer."

"Today a confrontation with these four elements awaits you. Only by concentrating, by gathering all your will, can you become a part of them. Only by understanding what they truly are can you gain power over them."

"Air. The source of breath. Begetter of whirlwinds," came the voice of another of the Great Mages. The face hidden by a hood did not let me tell who it was. He raised his magic staff. At once a wind rose, gathering strength, and in less than a minute it turned into a destructive hurricane. The mages remained motionless, while I barely had the strength to stay on my feet. The wind howled like a madman, trying to throw me out of the circle, yet I stood firm. Soon the wind died down.

"Earth," came the voice of the next mage. "Bearer of fruit. The rockfall in the mountains, the mudslide that kills villages."

In that very second the floor beneath me began to shake, trying to throw me off. And once more I had to summon all my will to stay on my feet. The mages remained motionless, while the earthquake thundered so that it seemed the whole tower was rocking. Which of it was real? Which was illusion? I did not understand.

"Water. Source of life. Cold ice," the next mage entered the fray. Around me the sea seemed to boil. Holding my breath, I stepped into the depths of the water, which in seconds changed from scalding hot to icy. It did not last long. The water receded, and I was left standing, soaked. The illusions, if that is what they were, were astonishingly convincing.

"Fire. Sustainer of life. Destroyer of all," the voice itself was cold, and with horror I recognised Catherine. The elf had no intention of going easy on me. Of those who failed the Test only a handful of ash remained, and it seemed this witch meant to burn me alive.

Walls of fire soared up around me. The deadly heat instantly dried my wet clothes, burned away all the air around me, and began to melt the ageless granite of the floor. I felt myself beginning to burn. My sleeves and hem caught fire, and I had to throw off all my clothes, remaining as naked as the day I was born. The flames roared, and I, eyes squeezed shut, kept standing in the circle, paying no heed to my skin as it blistered with burns. A foul smell of burning filled the hall.

In the flames, whose bright light pierced even my closed eyelids, I saw images of the past. My father, my mother. The dead man on the square. Hyster. The witch. The brigand chieftain. My teacher. My… first creation? The protector made of sticks?

The fire vanished as though it had never been. The Test was passed.

## 22 January 850

I sat in my chamber in Chasograd. Smaragd was away. For days now he and the other mages of the League's Conclave had been locked in long disputes, trying to decide where exactly Emperor Nezeb, ruler of the Xadagan Empire, would strike. His army of orcs seemed invincible, and the strength of one Great Mage with the allod under his command was not enough to repel such an onslaught.

The portals could open anywhere, and Scarge, like any other allod, could become a target.

I was busy with something else. Plates and gears were laid out before me. Taking a wrench in hand, I diligently tightened yet another washer. The work demanded perseverance and patience. Anyone who said I lacked them would have been put to shame had they seen me at work.

A huge frame, taller than a man, was filling with drive mechanisms and springs. A complex self-winding system would allow it to function on its own for a long time. But magic was needed too — chiefly because I did not want a mindless idol.

A little way off lay sticks darkened with age and a dried, shrivelled pumpkin. My old friend… A creature brought to life that had somehow acquired a semblance of a soul. I myself did not understand how I had managed back then to push that mysterious spark into the body of this half-golem. It had been hard to find these remains on the ashes of the witch's house. But my faithful friend had taken revenge, even if that was all the small measure of magic I had put into it could manage.

Now I planned to move it into this new dwelling. A difficult task, but an interesting one. Having finished assembling the body and gathered my concentration, I turned my inner sight deep into the broom handle. The spark flickered faintly, almost invisible to those who did not know where to look. Biting my lip, I began to pour my own strength into it, but it was not enough… Then I remembered the ritual. The elements I had had to pass through. The dragon's blood I drank afterwards, which still burns me from within, filling me with the greatest of gifts.

And I poured those memories into him. Into my protector. My guardian. My bogatyr.

Now the Spark will live in a new body. Mechanisms whirred, setting that mighty body in motion. It seemed that the unseeing gaze found mine, and I felt our bond, stretching across the years. The bogatyr reached out his mighty arms and held me tightly to him. My friend had returned.

## 25 September 897

The weather suits the occasion. A cold autumn rain, a sky covered with clouds, a grey day, the grey stone of graves. I am sad, though my mind understands that I have now taken another step towards my own greatness. The pupil of a Great Mage, who held the allod in his absence, has become its rightful sovereign.

Well, Smaragd will not be forgotten. What matters is not how he died, defending the Kanian lands from the Xadaganian advance; what matters is what he managed to accomplish. Chasograd is an example to all. They say the Arisen Zem, who only recently returned to our world, have begun rebuilding the Empire in their own way. They call it "technical progress", and, as merchants and spies report, the first fruits of this progress have already appeared.

Smaragd never spoke of it, but he knew that sooner or later we ourselves would have to follow a similar path. Now everyone must be convinced. Those moss-grown mages bound to their wands and books, those peasants who plough with share and mouldboard, those haughty elves who consider a thousand-year-old past the ideal.

## 5 March 910

Rahl-Scarge was burning, unable to withstand the onslaught of the accursed astral creatures that had fallen upon our allod.

I hardly knew what I was doing any more — all my strength had gone into shielding the city from a huge creature the size of a mountain, which kept pounding the surrounding fields, turning the solid earth into heaps of stone.

But the small vermin were even more dangerous — there were so many of them… They had broken through the perimeter, and now the blood froze in my veins at the screams that carried over the outer town, where the common folk had locked themselves in.

It seemed… it seemed that Scarge's end had come. What remained was to save those who could be saved. Gathering my will, I began to recall the portal spell. Open as many as possible, let those who could escape go… What else was there to do?

Sadly, most of my pupils were dead. They had fallen in the battle that began the moment those dreadful creatures appeared here… I thought feverishly about what else could be done. Not far from Yargrad there was an elven settlement, and their magister, Gustave de Grandeur, could help.

Rubbing my temples and concentrating, I sent him a mental message.

"Help me open portals. The people must be led out."

The answer came at once. Thoughts do not convey emotions, but I saw the arrogant bastard as clearly as if he stood before me.

"A great pity. I must lead my own people out."

"Curse it! There are very few of you, and among you a whole crowd of mages. Help me!"

"I am sorry."

"I beg you. Help! I am asking… I cannot manage it alone."

He did not even bother to answer. Collapsing onto the floor, I sobbed. From rage, from despair.

But time was running out. I got up and resolutely set off.

In front of the tower there was a small square. Opening the gates, I let in everyone who could get through. It remained to open a portal, though my strength was nearly spent.

Few were here. Frightened half to death, they now looked at me. Bagrena Zharova, Avdotya Belova. With little children clinging to their skirts. Gritting my teeth against the pain, I waved my hand, and a lilac radiance enveloped the space before the doors.

"Quickly! Into the portal!" I shouted with all my strength. The stream of people rushed into the haze that was their last hope. But there really was little time.

The huge creature made for the tower. With a single blow it swept away the top, turning the upper floors into heaps of rubble that came crashing down onto the people. I sent out a spell that covered them, but that effort cost me dearly. I felt as though something inside me had torn. Something ran down my cheek.

I kept holding both the portal and the shield. This drew the attention of those creatures, and they did everything to break through it. My sight was darkening, but I tried to maintain the spell. I felt the blood running from my eyes and ears dripping onto the ground. A little more. Not everyone had gone yet.

Darkness squeezed my head like an iron band. Losing consciousness, I cast the last of my strength into the portal. Suddenly a strong, firm hand caught me. Of course — the bogatyr could not forget me. He lifted me as if I were a feather, and I could not even order him to leave me on my allod. He stepped into the portal.

## 10 May 910

Politics, as always, is made in the capital. Alas, as always, they barely take me into account! And yet it was I who saved their marvellous Kvatoh when I took up the holding spell after the death of Tensess.

But the Conclave does not want to see me as the holder of this allod. As if they had a wide choice, ha! Catastrophe has befallen us; more than half the members of the Conclave are dead, and there are hardly many candidates.

Of course, it is a large allod, and I may not have the strength to hold all of it — this I admit. Well, they will have to make room; the capital has already fallen into the astral anyway, so new cities will have to be built. Half the allod is gone as if it had never been, and, most importantly, the least populated parts survived — the protected lake country and the wild bogs with the icy taiga. Those I can hold.

However, there is another way out. Old Skrakan proposed it to me. The former head of the Conclave, he now seems to run everything, or at least is trying to do something in this chaos. I am in his debt — it was he who once sent the company of heroes who rescued me from the witch.

Skrakan proposed an advantageous exchange: he will cede to me his own Umoir, the largest of the existing allods, held securely not only by spells but also by a number of powerful artefacts (as far as I understood from rumours, meteorite iron is somehow involved here).

He himself will move to Kvatoh, because the war with the demons requires his presence in the capital. And the creatures, although their first onslaught was repelled, have by no means disappeared. As I understand it, Skrakan has encountered these beings once before. Most curious; I want to get to the bottom of it.

Well, the capital is not for me (yet!), but Umoir looks like a tasty morsel too. I think I can bring a measure of progress to this backwater. The capital, the city of Plagot, is destroyed, and so is Rahl-Umoir, so this allod needs sweeping changes!

## 17 March 1005

For long years I endured all sorts of small fry who opposed my plans. Now everything will be different. Having received the rights of Viceroy of this allod, I will remake everything here as I please. Betting on the aristocracy was the right choice, and by making eyes at a number of local princes I managed to win their votes. The rabble put forward some petty merchant who could not be taken seriously. My victory was impressive.

But if the princes think they can dictate their will to me, they are deeply mistaken. None of them can be trusted, though I will do everything to make them feel prosperous and content. First of all, the elves must be got rid of — those scoundrels have long been undermining our trade. Let them get off my allod! And the Gibberlings too; they have no business here.

The next thing I plan to do is to take the local Church in hand. It seems Nadezhda Sialova very much wants to see herself as Prioress. And she really is far better suited to the role than the present hierarch, who does nothing. And on the allod vile heathens still breed, the people are superstitious and wild, and witches hold the forests and bogs under their sway. I swear I will watch them burn at the stake!

Faith is the key to power. I understood this when I saw how quickly the power of the believers in Tensess grew. If I establish faith in the Light on Umoir, its inhabitants will believe in me as well, and that will increase my strength. Sooner or later I will have to fight for power over the whole League, and then over Sarnaut, and I must be ready.

## 19 April 1013

The time has come to set about carrying out my long-held plan. For more than a hundred years now we have seen these beings, the astral demons, which possess considerable power. Clearly they are our enemies. Skrakan supposed that they come from another world, and even set out on an expedition fatal to himself and to Nezeb, to close some June gates he had supposedly learned about from Tka-Rik, another ancient mage.

However, the latest information shows that he was wrong. The demons are creatures of our world, offspring of the deep astral. And yet long ago Skrakan managed to summon one of these mighty creatures, a Demon more intelligent than the others.

That Demon was struck down by the four from Umoir. Shortly before these astral beings returned once more, three of the heroes fell into eternal sleep in the Mound. From Skrakan's notes I managed to learn that the Demon was buried there as well.

The corpse of such a creature (and it did not burn like the others) must be of particular interest. The demons draw their strength from the astral itself, and their destructive nature is its offspring. By studying the body of the demon, which has most likely been preserved in such conditions, I could understand a great deal about them — perhaps master the power of the astral in a new, unexplored way, learning to draw on it directly, without any devices.

This would lead to a new techno-magical revolution. Umoir would become the cradle of technology not only for backward Kania but for all of Sarnaut.

Superstitious bumpkins and benighted obscurantists warn me against "disturbing the peace of the dead, for an unheard-of evil may be awakened". Ha, what nonsense!

The Demon is long dead. I need only its body. Tomorrow I and my bogatyrs will set out for the Mound. The time has come for the excavation, and no one will stop us!
